The Blightborne have seeded a sickness across the galaxy, and you are the newest field epidemiologist on the Galactic Response Team. Every level is a small web of people. Lines are contacts. Each night the sick pass it on along those lines. Each day you get a few moves to stop it.

Galactic Outbreak is a turn-based puzzle game you can play in a few minutes, and it quietly teaches the real science of how outbreaks grow and how they are stopped.

HOW IT PLAYS
• Cut a red line to break a contact before the illness can cross it.
• Vaccinate a healthy person so the outbreak has nowhere to go.
• Quarantine a sick person so they cannot pass it on.
• On later planets, test people to find hidden carriers and hand out masks to lower the odds on every line.
• Tap Next day and watch what your choices did.

You win when nobody is sick. You lose if cases reach the cap. Moves are limited every day, so the puzzle is always the same question real outbreak teams face: where does one action do the most good?

WHAT YOU LEARN WITHOUT BEING LECTURED
• R, the number that says whether an outbreak is growing or shrinking. It is live on screen and drops the moment you act.
• Herd immunity, and why protecting the right people protects everyone behind them.
• Hubs and super-spreaders, and why one busy person matters more than ten quiet ones.
• Contact tracing and testing, when people can carry an illness before they feel sick.
• Masks, ring vaccination, shielding the vulnerable and flattening the curve.
• Why acting early beats acting hard.

Every level names the idea it teaches in one plain sentence. The invented strains (Cinderbloom, Gale Fever, Feastbane, Greylung and Voidcough) each spread the way a real disease does, and the game tells you which one.

WHAT IS IN THE GAME
• 80 levels across 8 gold and enamel planets, from the island towns of Mizuho to the storm world of Kaminari.
• Schools, festivals, markets, spaceports and healing halls, each with its own shape of outbreak and its own set of tools.
• A boss on every planet: a super-spreader carrier you have to find and stop.
• Dr. Amina Diallo, a veteran field epidemiologist, who briefs you before each level and has a dry word for you after it.
• A Daily puzzle that is the same for everyone that day, with a streak to keep and a result you can share.
• Up to three stars per level, bonus goals, gold to spend on power-ups and permanent upgrades, and supply chests along the way.
• A hint when you are stuck, and an undo for your last move.
• Gentle mode in Settings: slower spread and one extra use of each tool every day, for younger players or a calmer game. Stars still count.
• An original soundtrack, sound and vibration you can switch off one by one.

GOOD TO KNOW
• Plays offline. No account, no sign in, and your progress stays on your device.
• People in the game can die if an outbreak gets away from you. It is shown gently, never graphically, because that is what is at stake in the real thing.
• The game is a simplified model made to teach ideas. It is not medical advice.
• Free to play with short ads between levels, never during one. A one-time Remove Ads purchase turns them off for good.
